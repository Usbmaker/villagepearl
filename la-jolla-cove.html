La Jolla, California

La Jolla Cove

One of Southern California's most extraordinary marine environments — sea lions, leopard sharks, sea caves, and some of the clearest water on the California coast.

The Cove

A Marine Reserve Unlike Anywhere Else

La Jolla Cove is a small, sheltered inlet tucked below towering sandstone bluffs — and it is one of the most biologically rich spots on the California coast. The cove sits within the San Diego–La Jolla Underwater Park Ecological Reserve, a protected zone where fishing is prohibited and marine life thrives in remarkable abundance.

The result is an underwater world that feels almost tame by ocean standards: leopard sharks cruise the sandy shallows in summer, bright orange garibaldi dart through kelp forests, and sea lions haul out on the rocks in plain view of the clifftop park above.

The water at the Cove is famously clear — visibility can exceed 30 feet on calm days, making it one of the best shore-entry snorkeling and diving locations in Southern California. The reserve's protected status means the fish here are unafraid of humans, which makes for extraordinary in-water encounters.

The surrounding bluffs, accessible via the Children's Pool seawall and the cliffside path, offer some of the most dramatic coastal scenery in the region — and views of the open Pacific that stretch all the way to the horizon on clear days.

For Guests

What to Do at La Jolla Cove

🤿

Snorkeling

Enter from the small sandy beach at the Cove and fin north along the kelp. Garibaldi, sheephead, and bat rays are common sightings. The protected status means the fish are curious rather than wary.

🌊

Scuba Diving

La Jolla Cove is one of the top shore-entry dive sites in California. The kelp forest extends north to deeper reef systems. La Jolla Cove Dive & Kayak rents gear and runs guided dives from the beach.

🦭

Sea Lions & Seals

California sea lions are a permanent fixture on the rocks surrounding the Cove. Harbor seals gather at Children's Pool (a short walk south). Both are wild animals — admire from a respectful distance.

🚣

Kayaking & Paddleboarding

Launch a kayak from the Cove beach for a self-guided tour of the sea caves — the largest is La Jolla Cave, accessible by both water and a staircase from the street above (La Jolla Cave Shop).

🦈

Leopard Sharks

July through October, leopard sharks aggregate in the warm shallow water off La Jolla Shores (a 10-min walk north of the Cove). Swimming alongside these completely harmless sharks is a bucket-list experience.

🦅

Cliff Walk & Views

The clifftop path from the Cove park toward Children's Pool offers unobstructed Pacific views. At sunset, the sandstone bluffs glow amber and the sea lions bark below — one of La Jolla's great free experiences.

A Local Secret

The Seven Sea Caves

The sandstone bluffs of La Jolla conceal a series of seven sea caves carved by centuries of Pacific waves. The largest and most accessible is Sunny Jim Cave — the only one reachable by land, via a 145-step staircase that descends through a tunnel to an underground viewing platform.

The entrance fee is minimal and the experience is genuinely remarkable — the cave opens onto the ocean through a natural arch, and at high tide the water surges in with a deep, resonant boom.

The remaining six caves — including Clam's Cave, Little Sister, and Shopping Cart Cave — are best explored by kayak. La Jolla Cove Dive & Kayak offers guided tours that take you through the caves one by one, with narration on the geology and the local sea lion population.

The caves are visible from the cliffside path above — look for the pale green water where light reflects off the submerged sandy floor inside each cave.

Practical Guide

Know Before You Go

Best Times to Visit

  • Early morning (7–9am): Calmest water and fewest visitors. Best visibility for snorkeling.
  • July–October: Peak leopard shark season at La Jolla Shores. Also warmest water (65–70°F).
  • Low tide: Sea caves are most dramatic and kayak tours operate more easily in calmer surge.
  • Sunset year-round: The cliff walk turns golden and the sea lion chorus hits its peak energy.

What to Bring

  • Snorkel gear: La Jolla Cove Dive & Kayak rents snorkel sets right at the beach. Reservations recommended in summer.
  • Wetsuit: Water is cold (58–68°F year-round). Even in summer, a 3mm suit makes snorkeling far more comfortable for extended sessions.
  • Reef-safe sunscreen only: Required in the ecological reserve. Standard sunscreen is harmful to the marine environment here.
  • Walking shoes: The cliffside paths are paved but the Cove beach entry has slippery rocks near the waterline.

Nearby essentials: George's at the Cove (rooftop dining, sunset views), The Cottage La Jolla (breakfast), and a cluster of dive shops and coffee spots along Prospect Street — all within a few minutes' walk of the Cove entrance.

From The Village Pearl

A 17-Minute Walk to the Cove

The Village Pearl sits in the heart of La Jolla Village, and the Cove is an easy walk downhill and north through the Village's most scenic streets.

1

Head west toward Girard Avenue

Walk through the Village — Girard is the main commercial street lined with galleries, cafés, and boutiques.

2

Turn left on Girard, then right on Prospect Street

Prospect Street runs along the clifftops — the ocean comes into view as the street bends north.

3

Continue north on Coast Boulevard

The clifftop park on your left overlooks the Cove. The La Jolla Cave Shop (Sunny Jim Cave entrance) is on your right.

4

Descend the steps to the Cove beach

Total walk: approximately 17 minutes. The snorkel rental shop and kayak tours launch from this small beach.

Distance: ~0.9 miles  ·  Walk time: 17 minutes  ·  Terrain: Village streets, mostly flat with gentle descent to coast
