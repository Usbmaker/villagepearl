Windansea Beach

Where the Pacific meets raw rock and surf culture runs deep — a short stroll from The Village Pearl.

The Beach

A Living Legend of Southern California Surf

Windansea Beach is one of San Diego's most iconic — and most untamed — stretches of coastline. Unlike the wide sandy expanses further north, Windansea is defined by dramatic reef formations, surging shore break, and the kind of raw Pacific energy that has drawn surfers, artists, and adventurers for generations.

The beach takes its name from a roadhouse called "The Wind 'n Sea," which stood here in the early 20th century. Today, the name lives on as shorthand for a particular San Diego spirit: relaxed but serious, beautiful but unforgiving.

The famous Windansea Surf Shack — a small thatched palapa that has stood on the beach since 1946 — is recognized as a San Diego Historical Landmark. It shelters surfers checking the break, and has served as an unofficial gathering place for the local surf community for nearly 80 years.

The beach is also home to Simmons Reef, a world-class right-hand point break that produces powerful, hollow waves on south and northwest swells — beloved by experienced surfers and fascinating to watch from the shore.

For Guests

What to Do at Windansea

🌊

Watch the Surf

Even if you don't surf, watching experienced locals navigate Simmons Reef is mesmerizing. Morning sessions in summer produce glassy, long rides. Find a seat on the flat rocks above the shack.

🐚

Explore the Tidepools

The rocky reef at the south end reveals vibrant tidepool communities at low tide — sea urchins, hermit crabs, anemones, and the occasional octopus. Check tide charts and go within an hour of low tide.

🌅

Sunset from the Rocks

The flat reef formations north of the shack are perfect for watching the sun drop into the Pacific. Arrive 30 minutes early to claim a perch — it's a local ritual, and a magical one.

🏊

Swimming

The small sandy crescent north of the main reef is calmer and better for swimming when conditions allow. Always check the lifeguard flags — Windansea's shore break can be deceptively powerful.

🤿

Snorkeling the Reef

On calm days, the shallow reef offers surprisingly good snorkeling — kelp forests, garibaldi, and bat rays. Enter from the north end of the beach when the surf is small (under 2 feet).

📸

Photography

The surf shack, dramatic rocks, and golden light make Windansea one of the most photographed beaches in San Diego. Morning fog burns off by 10am, leaving perfect soft light over the reef.

Practical Guide

Know Before You Go

Best Times to Visit

  • Early morning (7–9am): Glassy conditions, empty beach, and soft light for photography.
  • Sunset (year-round): The most spectacular light, especially in fall and winter when the sun tracks south.
  • Low tide (–1 to +1 ft): Best for tidepools and reef snorkeling. Check tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov.
  • Summer mornings: South swells produce the beach's best surf — and best spectator sessions.

What to Bring

  • Water shoes or reef sandals: The rock entries can be slippery and sharp.
  • Snorkel gear: Rental shops in La Jolla Village are 10 minutes away.
  • Sunscreen: The reef reflects sun — burn happens fast, even on overcast mornings.
  • Cash for parking: Street parking is free along Neptune Place. Arrive before 9am on weekends.

Safety note: Windansea's shore break is powerful and the reef is hard. The waves that look manageable from the rocks can knock adults down. Swim between the flags, and when in doubt, watch from the rocks — it's equally spectacular.

From The Village Pearl

A 15-Minute Walk to the Shore

No car needed. Start at the corner of Pearl Street and Girard Avenue — Windansea is a straight shot south and west through the Village and down to the shore.

1

Head south on Girard Avenue from Pearl Street

Girard is La Jolla's main commercial corridor — grab a coffee at one of the cafés along the way as you head toward the coast.

2

Turn right (west) on Nautilus Street

Nautilus runs straight toward the ocean — the street descends gently and you'll get your first glimpse of the Pacific ahead.

3

Continue west to Neptune Place and turn left

Neptune Place runs along the bluff above the beach — you'll hear the surf before you see it. Follow it south a short block.

4

Arrive at the iconic Windansea Surf Shack

Total walk: approximately 15 minutes. The thatched shack is your landmark — the beach and reef spread out in both directions.

Start: Pearl St & Girard Ave  ·  Distance: ~0.7 miles  ·  Walk time: 15 minutes  ·  Terrain: Flat through Village, gentle descent to Neptune Place
